# Research review — what is wrong first

Research date: **6 September 2026**. Scope: the plans in this repository, not an implementation audit. No project code was produced or run. Read this before the proposed build specifications in plans/17–21. Those documents are amendments for review, not silent replacements of previous decisions.

Evidence vocabulary follows [sources.md](sources.md): **Verified** means direct support for a limited claim; **Strong inference** identifies an argument joining evidence; **Speculation** stays out of the pitch. Mathematical counterexamples below are **design reasoning, high confidence**, not a claim that a paper tested Truss. A proposed protocol is not a verified implementation.

## The verdict

1. **I3 does not follow from the existing protocol.** Receipt-started TTLs, concurrent lease generations, changing caps and unreserved fallback floors each break it independently. A scalar allocator test does not test this invariant.
2. **The fairness theorem is misstated.** The intended rule is weighted max-min fairness of *surplus above floors*, for a fixed snapshot and fixed weights. It is not ordinary leximin of total watts, a deadline guarantee, or proof of fairness over time.
3. **The novelty claim is too broad.** PEM is important, but EEBus, dynamic operating envelopes, FlexOffers and household-level distributed optimisation are at least as important to the claimed combination. “The layer nobody owns” should be cut.
4. **The privacy claim exceeds the architecture.** Device topics, a whole-site logger and a device inspector export the list outside the household. Aggregate offers also disclose patterns. Data minimisation is defensible; “not nearly well enough to know anything about the people” is not.
5. **The proposed 48-hour build is too large for this team.** The first likely failure is contract and safety integration, before the optional solver. The full React site makes the later schedule worse. This is an engineering estimate, not a sourced performance claim.

## The three headline technical claims

### Leases: the citation is good; the transfer is incomplete

S23 explicitly discusses the bad combination of a fast server clock and slow client clock. A monotonic clock prevents backward jumps; it does not bound rate error, suspension, process pauses or actuation delay. Recovery waiting works only when the waited interval bounds **all old usable authority**, including authority still travelling through queues.

The paper does not turn cooperative appliances into an enforceable electrical limiter. A process killed before its expiry callback cannot execute that callback. An appliance permitted to refuse shutdown can keep drawing after its member's lease ends. Both require a narrower fault model or an independent enforcement path. See [the attack traces and repair](../plans/18-protocol-repair.md).

### Water-filling: keep a theorem, state the right variables

Own derivation, high confidence. Let baseline `f_i`, useful ceiling `u_i`, fixed positive weight `w_i`, surplus `x_i = b_i − f_i`. With continuous divisible watts and one shared constraint, weighted filling gives:

`x_i = min(u_i − f_i, w_i × λ)` with the largest λ consistent with the surplus pool.

It lexicographically maximises the sorted vector **`x_i / w_i`**. It does not maximise sorted `b_i`.

Counterexample without debt: floors `(100, 0)`, useful ceilings `(200, 200)`, total capacity `200`. The proposed algorithm returns `(150, 50)`. Total-watt leximin returns `(100, 100)`. With zero floors, capacity `90` and weights `(2, 1)`, weighted filling gives `(60, 30)`, while ordinary max-min gives `(45, 45)`.

The pseudocode in 07 repeatedly scans and updates active members. If one member saturates per round, its work is `n + (n−1) + … + 1 = O(n²)`. Sort breakpoints `(u_i−f_i)/w_i`, maintain the active weight sum, then materialise budgets once to achieve `O(n log n)`. Do not claim microseconds for five thousand members before measuring.

Floors plus discrete appliances make an additional distinction: a feasible budget need not be fully usable. Two 500 W indivisible loads under a 600 W shared surplus can each receive 300 W and neither can start. Allocation fairness is not delivered-energy fairness. No weight cap fixes this.

### PEM: nearest neighbour in packets, not the only nearest neighbour

“Fleet of like devices” is contradicted by S25's heterogeneous-resource scope. The particular packet controller is device-oriented, but that is no basis for claiming the entire literature excludes households; there is also [residential PEM work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08535). Do not cite that paper's cost reductions as Truss evidence.

The narrower useful comparison is: PEM's probabilistic local requests and packets versus Truss's explicit per-household baseline, deterministic snapshot allocation and audited lease reservation. In the chapter inspected, communication faults are outside the evaluation. That distinguishes the experiment we propose; it does not prove that no later PEM paper studies faults.

There is no evidence here that Truss is worthless because components exist. There is enough evidence to stop defending the project by denying those components exist elsewhere.

## Fairness under mistakes and adversarial offers

The 2.0 cap is defensible as a **declared policy bound**, not a result derived from Ostrom or a welfare optimum. In a fixed continuous snapshot, two unsaturated members receive surplus in their weight ratio, at most 2:1. A positive surplus pool gives active members a positive continuous share. With zero surplus, integer rounding, or indivisible jobs, that is not a no-starvation theorem.

Failure modes: persistent inflated useful demand earns debt without a real job; large homes accumulate more Wh; churn resets or duplicates identity; persistent overload saturates all weights and removes differentiation; replayed offers double-count debt; unused grants accumulate credit if debt uses requests minus observed draw; debt never repays if it only increases; variable ticks change results if elapsed time is ignored. Changing offers/weights invalidates fixed-snapshot monotonicity arguments. None is fixed by writing “audited” next to a member-authored number.

**Recommended replacement:** registered baselines plus equal-surplus filling as the hour-6 baseline; a bounded *service deficit relative to an equal-surplus reference* as an optional hour-18 enhancement. Explain it as: “Everyone keeps an agreed baseline; extra power is shared, with a limited catch-up credit for service previously withheld.” Details, formulas and costs are in 18. This removes the unsupported promise that aggregate scarcity debt will be repaid.

**Floor fraud has no purely informational solution here.** Two households emitting the same numbers, one honestly and one dishonestly, are indistinguishable to this coordinator. Register one identity per household and an agreed baseline/envelope before admitting it; do not let ordinary offers increase that baseline. A request above it is a policy conflict for review, not silently clipped “safe” demand. Metering can verify draw, not medical need. Do not request medical diagnoses or penalise protected loads automatically. A malicious actuator remains outside the protocol's enforcement assumptions.

- Actual team skill distribution, available OS versions, router ownership, actuator timing, clock drift or benchmark latency.
- Current organiser dates, advance-specification permission or judging weights. The team's stated restrictions govern this output. Retyping an impermissible advance artifact would not make it permissible; confirm the preparation boundary with organisers.
